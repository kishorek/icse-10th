## Approved complex-term table — approved 2026-11-21 (GATE 2)

Word-parts are decoding aids; the exam-safe definition is the wording to reproduce.

| Term | Breakdown | Plain-language gloss | Exam-safe definition | Lesson | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rigid body | *rigid* (stiff) + *body* | A body that keeps its shape and size | A body whose shape and size do not change when forces act on it | 1 | An ideal model, not "hard" |
| translational motion | *trans* (across) + *lation* (carrying) | The whole body is carried from place to place | Motion in which the body moves along a straight path in the direction of the force | 1 | Not "translated text" |
| rotational motion | *rota* (wheel) + *-tional* | Turning about a fixed point like a wheel | Motion in which the body turns about a fixed axis, so its particles move in circles about that axis | 1 | Rotation is about an axis |
| pivot | *pivot* (hinge pin) | The fixed point a body turns about | The fixed point about which a body rotates | 1 | The pivot itself does not move |
| moment of force | *moment* (turning effect in physics) + *of force* | How strongly a force twists something | The turning effect of a force about a pivot: force × perpendicular distance of its line of action from the pivot | 1 | Everyday "moment" means an instant in time |
| torque | *torquere* (to twist) | A twisting effect | Another name for the moment of a force | 1 | Same quantity as moment of force here |
| perpendicular distance | *per* (through) + *pendiculus* (plumb line) | Distance measured at 90° to the force | The shortest distance from the pivot to the line of action, measured at right angles to it | 1 | Not the distance along the force |
| line of action | *line* + *of action* | The straight line a force acts along | The straight line through the point of application, along the direction of the force | 1 | Extend it when needed |
| clockwise / anticlockwise moment | *clock* + *wise* (manner) | Turning like clock hands, or the opposite | Clockwise turns in the clock-hand direction and is taken negative; anticlockwise is opposite and taken positive | 1 | A stated convention, not a law of nature |
| couple | *copula* (link) via Old French *couple* (pair) | A matched pair of twisting forces | Two equal, opposite and parallel forces whose lines of action do not coincide | 2 | The lines must be separate |
| couple arm | *arm* (lever distance) | The gap between the two force lines | The perpendicular distance between the lines of action of the two forces of a couple | 2 | A perpendicular gap, not the bar length |
| equilibrium | *aequi* (equal) + *libra* (balance) | Balanced, nothing changing | A body is in equilibrium when the forces on it produce no change in its state of rest or of uniform motion | 2 | "Balanced forces" is the cause; "no change" is the test |
| static equilibrium | *statikos* (causing to stand) + *equilibrium* | Balanced while at rest | Equilibrium when the body remains at rest | 2 | Book on a table |
| dynamic equilibrium | *dynamis* (power) + *equilibrium* | Balanced while moving steadily | Equilibrium when the body continues in the same state of uniform motion | 2 | Raindrop at terminal velocity |
| resultant | *resultare* (to spring back) | The single overall effect | A single force that produces the same effect as two or more forces acting together | 2 | Word-parts do not predict the meaning |
| principle of moments | *principle* (starting rule) + *moments* | The balance rule for turning effects | For a body in equilibrium about a pivot, sum of anticlockwise moments = sum of clockwise moments | 3 | Take moments about the same pivot |
| centre of gravity | *centre* (compass point) + *gravity* (*gravis*, heavy) | The point where the whole weight seems to act | The point about which the algebraic sum of the moments of the weights of all particles of the body is zero; the whole weight may be taken to act there | 4 | It can lie outside the material |
| plumb line | *plumbum* (lead) + *line* | A thread with a lead bob hanging straight down | A fine thread with a small heavy bob, used to mark the vertical direction | 4 | It marks vertical |
| lamina | *lamina* (thin plate) | A thin flat sheet | A thin flat sheet of material of uniform thickness | 4 | A card shape, not a block |
| uniform circular motion | *uniform* (*unus* + *forma*) + *circular* (*circulus*) | Same speed, round path | Motion of a particle with constant speed along a circular path | 5 | Speed is constant; velocity is not |
| speed vs velocity | *speed* (quickness) / *velocitas* (swiftness) | How fast, and how fast in which direction | Speed is distance ÷ time (scalar); velocity is displacement ÷ time (vector) | 5 | Not synonyms in physics |
| tangent | *tangere* (to touch) | A line just touching the circle | A straight line touching the circle at one point; instantaneous velocity is along the tangent | 5 | Gives direction, not the path |
| acceleration | *ad* (towards) + *celerare* (to quicken) | Rate of change of velocity | The rate of change of velocity; produced by a net force and directed along it | 5 | Turning is acceleration at constant speed |
| centripetal force | *centri* (centre) + *petere* (to seek) | Centre-seeking force | The force directed towards the centre of the circular path that keeps a body moving in that path | 5 | A role played by tension, gravity or electrostatic force |
| centrifugal force | *centri* (centre) + *fugere* (to flee) | Centre-fleeing force | A force equal in magnitude to the centripetal force but directed away from the centre, assumed by an observer in the rotating frame; fictitious | 5 | Not the reaction to centripetal force |
| fictitious force | *ficticius* (artificial) | An invented force used in a spinning frame | A force assumed to explain motion in a non-inertial (rotating) frame; it has no real agent | 5 | No agent, but not unimportant |
| vector / scalar | *vehere* (to carry) / *scala* (scale) | Has direction / has size only | A vector has magnitude and direction (force, moment, velocity); a scalar has magnitude only (speed, mass) | 1 | Moment's unit is N m but it is not work |
| kilogram-force (kgf) | *kilogram* + *force* | The pull of gravity on 1 kg | The gravitational unit of force equal to the weight of a 1 kg mass; 1 kgf = 9.8 N | 1 | Not an SI unit |
| dyne | *dynamis* (force) | The CGS unit of force | The CGS unit of force; 1 N = 10⁵ dyne | 1 | Convert before calculating in SI |
