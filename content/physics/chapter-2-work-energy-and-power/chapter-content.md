## Approved complex-term table — approved 2026-11-21 (GATE 2)

Word-parts are decoding aids; the exam-safe definition is the wording to reproduce.

| Term | Breakdown | Plain-language gloss | Exam-safe definition | Lesson | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| work | No useful modern word-part split | Physics work happens only when a force moves a body | Work is the product of force and the displacement of the point of application in the direction of the force; `W = Fs cos θ`; scalar; SI unit joule | 1 | Everyday "work" means effort; effort alone is not physics work |
| displacement | *dis-* (away) + *place* + *-ment* (result) | How far, and in which direction, the position has changed | The change in position of a body in a stated direction; a vector | 1 | Not the same as distance travelled |
| scalar | Latin *scalaris* (of a ladder/scale) | A quantity with size only | A physical quantity having magnitude but no direction | 1 | Word-parts do not predict the meaning |
| vector | Latin *vector* (carrier), from *vehere* (to carry) | A quantity with size and direction | A physical quantity having both magnitude and direction | 1 | Word-parts do not predict the meaning |
| component | *com-* (together) + *ponere* (to place) | The part of a slanted force or displacement along a chosen direction | The effective part of a vector along a given direction | 1 | A component is not an extra force |
| perpendicular | *per-* (through) + *pendiculus* (plumb line) | At right angles | A line or distance measured at 90° to another line or direction | 1 | "Perpendicular" does not always mean vertical |
| cosine | *co-* (complement) + *sinus* (bay/fold) | The ratio used to find the part next to an angle | In a right triangle, cosine = adjacent side ÷ hypotenuse | 1 | Do not decode from the word-parts; use the ratio |
| dot product | *dot* (symbol) + *product* | A way of multiplying vectors that gives a scalar | The scalar product of force and displacement: `F S cos θ` | 1 | The result is a scalar, not a vector |
| joule | Named after James Prescott Joule | The SI unit of work and energy | 1 joule = 1 newton × 1 metre | 2 | Named unit; not related to "jewel" |
| erg | Greek *ergon* (work) | The CGS unit of work and energy | 1 erg = 1 dyne × 1 centimetre; `1 J = 10⁷ erg` | 2 | A very small unit |
| dyne | Greek *dynamis* (force) | The CGS unit of force | 1 dyne is the CGS force unit; `1 N = 10⁵ dyne` | 2 | Not an SI unit |
| kilogram-force (kgf) | *kilogram* + *force* | The pull of gravity on a 1 kg mass | Gravitational force unit; `1 kgf = 9.8 N` | 2 | Not an SI unit |
| power | Latin *posse* (to be able); no useful modern split | How quickly work is done | The rate of doing work: `P = W/t`; scalar; SI unit watt | 3 | Power is not the same as energy or work |
| watt | Named after James Watt | The SI unit of power | 1 watt = 1 joule per second | 3 | Named unit |
| horsepower | *horse* + *power* | A larger, non-SI unit of power | `1 HP = 746 W` | 3 | Not the literal power of one horse; it is a defined unit |
| energy | *en-* (in) + *ergon* (work) | The stored ability to do work | The energy of a body is its capacity to do work; scalar; SI unit joule | 4 | Energy is not force |
| watt-hour | *watt* + *hour* | Energy used by 1 W in 1 hour | `1 Wh = 3600 J` | 4 | A unit of energy, not power |
| kilowatt-hour | *kilo-* (thousand) + *watt* + *hour* | Energy used by 1 kW in 1 hour | `1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J` | 4 | A unit of energy, not power |
| calorie | Latin *calor* (heat) | Heat needed to warm 1 g of water by 1°C | Heat energy raising 1 g of water from 14.5°C to 15.5°C; `1 cal = 4.18 J` | 4 | Food "Calorie" usually means 1000 cal |
| electron volt | *electron* + *volt* | The tiny energy unit used for atomic particles | Energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 V; `1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J` | 4 | A unit of energy, not voltage |
| potential energy | *potential* (stored/possible) + *energy* | Energy stored because of position or shape | Energy possessed by a body by virtue of its changed position or configuration | 5 | "Potential" means stored here, not guaranteed to happen |
| gravitational potential energy | *gravitas* (weight) + *potential* + *energy* | Energy stored by lifting a body | Energy due to height in a gravitational field; near Earth, `U = mgh` | 5 | Its value depends on the chosen zero level |
| elastic potential energy | *elastic* (springy) + *potential* + *energy* | Energy stored in a stretched or compressed shape | Energy stored in a body because its configuration is changed | 5 | The body must be able to recover its shape |
| kinetic energy | Greek *kinētikos* (of motion), from *kinein* (to move) | Energy a body has because it is moving | Energy possessed by a body by virtue of its motion; `K = ½mv²` | 5 | Depends on the square of speed |
| translational kinetic energy | *trans-* (across) + *latus* (carried) + *kinetic* + *energy* | Energy of straight-line motion | Kinetic energy due to motion along a straight line | 5 | Not "translated" energy |
| rotational kinetic energy | Latin *rota* (wheel) + *-tional* + *kinetic* + *energy* | Energy of spinning motion | Kinetic energy due to rotation about an axis | 5 | The whole body need not change position |
| vibrational kinetic energy | Latin *vibrare* (to shake) + *-tional* + *kinetic* + *energy* | Energy of to-and-fro motion | Kinetic energy due to vibration about a mean position | 5 | The motion repeatedly reverses |
| work-energy theorem | *work* + *energy* + *theorem* (proved statement) | The net work changes the kinetic energy | The net work done on a body equals its change in kinetic energy: `W = K_f − K_i` | 5 | Use the net work, not one selected force |
| momentum | Latin *momentum* (movement), from *movere* (to move) | The "quantity of motion", `mv` | The product of mass and velocity; a vector; `p = mv` | 5 | Momentum and kinetic energy are different quantities |
| retardation | *re-* (back) + *tardus* (slow) | Slowing down | The rate of decrease of velocity; acceleration opposing the motion | 5 | Its sign depends on the chosen positive direction |
| mechanical energy | Greek *mēkhanē* (machine) + *-ical* + *energy* | The energy of motion plus stored energy | The sum of kinetic and potential energy of a body | 5 | Mechanical energy is conserved only when friction and air resistance are negligible |
| geothermal energy | *geo* (earth) + *therme* (heat) + *energy* | Heat from inside the Earth | Energy released from the Earth's interior, often as steam or hot water | 6 | Not energy from sunlight |
| transformation | *trans-* (across) + *forma* (form) | Changing from one form to another | Conversion of energy from one form to another | 6 | Energy is not created or destroyed in the change |
| conservation | *con-* (together) + *servare* (keep) | The total stays the same | Principle: energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it only changes form | 7 | Conservation of mechanical energy is a narrower special case |
| dissipation | Latin *dissipare* (to scatter) | Energy spreading out and becoming less useful | Conversion of energy into less useful forms, usually heat, and transfer to the surroundings | 7 | The energy is not destroyed |
| degraded energy | *de-* (down) + *gradus* (step) | Energy that can no longer do the intended job | Energy in a less useful form, unavailable for the original purpose | 7 | "Degraded" does not mean "no energy left" |
| pendulum | Latin *pendulus* (hanging), from *pendere* (to hang) | A hanging bob that swings | A small heavy bob suspended from a fixed support so that it can swing to and fro | 7 | The simple-pendulum treatment here is qualitative |
| oscillation | Latin *oscillare* (to swing) | Swinging back and forth | To-and-fro motion about a mean position | 7 | Not every vibration is visible |
