**Date:** 20 November 2026
**Status:** Approved by user in chat
**Delivery:** Vue 3 + Vite single-page application, static build output for GitHub Pages
**Syllabus:** CISCE ICSE Chemistry (Science Paper 2), examination year 2027

---

## 1. Context and assumptions

1. The user asked for “Chemistry - Organic Chemistry chapter 1” covering:
   - What are organic compounds?
   - Why is carbon the central element of organic chemistry?
   - Basic difference between organic and inorganic compounds.
   - Examples of everyday organic compounds.
   - Importance of organic compounds in everyday life.
2. The user approved a scope of **the five points plus the minimum carbon basics** needed to explain why carbon is central.
3. The examination year is taken as **2027**, the current ICSE cycle at the time of writing. The 2026-27 syllabus retains the same 9.1 introduction wording.
4. CISCE does not prescribe a Chemistry textbook. Textbook-specific page mapping is therefore **unverified** and is not claimed.
5. All examination-style questions are **original practice questions**, not board past-paper questions.
6. The chapter is a standalone introduction. It must not pretend to cover the whole Organic Chemistry unit (hydrocarbons, nomenclature, homologous series, alcohols, carboxylic acids).

---

## 2. Scope

### In scope

- The meaning and origin of organic chemistry.
- The modern definition of organic compounds and the standard carbon-containing exceptions.
- Carbon’s tetravalency and catenation, with a preview of multiple bonds, chains, rings, and isomerism.
- A functional comparison of organic and inorganic compounds, including exceptions.
- Everyday examples and importance.
- Quick checks, memory aids, revision, and a chapter assessment.

### Out of scope

- Detailed IUPAC nomenclature.
- Homologous-series calculations.
- Preparation and properties of alkanes, alkenes, alkynes, alcohols, and carboxylic acids.
- Reaction mechanisms.
- Full treatment of benzene chemistry; only the ring outline is previewed.

---

## 3. Learning outcomes

By the end of the chapter, the student can:

1. Define organic chemistry and organic compounds.
2. Explain why organic chemistry is studied separately.
3. Describe the historical shift from the vital-force idea to laboratory synthesis, using urea as the standard example.
4. Explain carbon’s central role using tetravalency, catenation, multiple bonding, and isomerism.
5. Distinguish organic from inorganic compounds using bonding, melting/boiling behaviour, solubility, conductivity, and combustion.
6. Identify carbon-containing compounds that are conventionally classified as inorganic.
7. Give everyday examples of organic compounds in fuels, food, clothing, medicine, plastics, cleaning products, and agriculture.
8. Explain the importance and some environmental consequences of organic compounds.
9. Answer recall, reasoning, comparison, diagram, and application questions on the introduction.

## 5. Chapter architecture

1. **Chapter orientation**
   - Chapter identity and essential question.
   - “What you will learn” outcomes.
   - Prerequisites: elements, compounds, covalent bonding, valency, electron configuration.
   - Concept map.
   - Syllabus coverage and honest scope note.
   - Estimated study time: 45–60 minutes, labelled as an estimate.

2. **Lesson 1 — What are organic compounds?**
   - Opening puzzle: “Why does a candle burn away, while a steel nail does not?”
   - Intuitive explanation.
   - Vital-force theory and Wöhler’s 1828 urea synthesis.
   - Modern definition.
   - Carbon-containing exceptions: CO, CO₂, carbonates, bicarbonates, cyanides, carbides.
   - Formal exam-ready definition.
   - Common misconception.
   - Quick check.

3. **Lesson 2 — Why carbon is the central element**
   - Carbon: atomic number 6, electronic configuration 2,4.
   - Tetravalency: four covalent bonds.
   - Catenation: carbon bonds to carbon; chains and rings.
   - Single, double, triple bonds.
   - Straight, branched, cyclic skeletons; benzene ring preview.
   - Isomerism preview: same formula, different structure.
   - Why silicon does not match carbon (optional enrichment; brief).
   - Two labs: bond builder and catenation explorer.
   - Quick checks.

4. **Lesson 3 — Organic vs inorganic compounds**
   - Comparison table across origin, bonding, composition, melting/boiling, solubility, conductivity, combustion, reaction rate.
   - “Like dissolves like”.
   - Exceptions and nuances.
   - Sorter lab with CO₂ and CaCO₃ traps.
   - Quick checks.

5. **Lesson 4 — Everyday organic compounds**
   - Fuels: LPG, petrol, diesel, natural gas, biogas.
   - Food: carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins.
   - Clothing: cotton cellulose, wool/silk proteins, polyester.
   - Medicine: aspirin, paracetamol.
   - Plastics and materials: polythene, PVC, polystyrene.
   - Cleaning: soap, detergent, ethanol.
   - Agriculture: urea.
   - Explorer lab.

6. **Lesson 5 — Why organic compounds matter**
   - Life and biochemistry.
   - Food and agriculture.
   - Health and medicine.
   - Materials, clothing, shelter.
   - Energy and transport.
   - Economy and employment.
   - Environment: plastic pollution, combustion emissions, sustainable alternatives.
   - Importance map and quick check.

7. **Memory workshop**
   - Acrostic: **“Tiny Carbon Makes Isomers”**
     - **T**etravalency
     - **C**atenation
     - **M**ultiple bonds
     - **I**somerism
   - Full word-to-item mapping and recall prompt.
   - Comparison story for organic vs inorganic (optional).
   - Flashcards.

8. **Revision centre**
   - Mind map.
   - Key definitions.
   - Comparison table.
   - Important formulae and structures.
   - Common mistakes.
   - Acrostic.
   - Rapid revision sheet.
   - Active-recall flashcards.

9. **Chapter assessment**
   - 24 original questions, roughly 39 marks.
   - Formats: single choice, multiple select, true/false with justification, fill in the blank, short answer, long answer, matching, and application/diagram-style prompts.

## 6. Interaction specifications

### 6.1 Carbon bond builder

- **Learning objective:** show that carbon forms four covalent bonds and can share 1, 2, or 3 pairs.
- **Student action:** choose methane, ethene, or ethyne; toggle bond order; reset.
- **Visible response:** SVG carbon centre, hydrogen atoms, bond lines; electron-pair count; formula.
- **Explanation:** single bond = 1 shared pair; double = 2; triple = 3. Carbon still has four bonds in total.
- **Accessibility:** buttons with text labels; SVG has a text summary; no drag required.

### 6.2 Catenation explorer

- **Learning objective:** show carbon’s self-linking into chains and rings.
- **Student action:** slider from 1 to 6 carbons; choose straight, branched, or ring.
- **Visible response:** SVG skeleton updates; molecular formula for the straight alkane updates; ring mode shows closed loop.
- **Explanation:** carbon can link to carbon indefinitely; this creates the huge variety of organic compounds.
- **Accessibility:** range input with visible value; text description updates; reset button.

### 6.3 Organic/inorganic sorter

- **Learning objective:** classify examples and confront the “contains carbon = organic” misconception.
- **Student action:** click a chip, then click a bin; or use keyboard buttons.
- **Visible response:** chip moves, feedback appears, score updates.
- **Items:** glucose, methane, ethanol, urea, polythene (organic); sodium chloride, copper(II) sulphate, carbon dioxide, calcium carbonate, sodium cyanide (inorganic).
- **Explanation:** CO₂, carbonates, and cyanides contain carbon but are conventionally inorganic.
- **Accessibility:** every action is a button; feedback is text, not colour-only.

### 6.4 Everyday organics explorer

- **Learning objective:** connect organic chemistry to daily life.
- **Student action:** select an item from a grid.
- **Visible response:** detail panel shows the item, compound class, example formula, and use.
- **Items:** LPG cylinder, cooking oil, cotton shirt, plastic bottle, aspirin strip, soap bar, urea bag, petrol pump.
- **Accessibility:** buttons with labels; detail panel uses a live region.

### 6.5 Flashcards

- **Learning objective:** active recall.
- **Student action:** flip card, next/previous, shuffle.
- **Visible response:** question side and answer side.
- **Accessibility:** buttons; card state announced in text.

### 6.6 Assessment

- **Learning objective:** validate all outcomes.
- **Student action:** answer, submit, reveal marking points for written items, restart.
- **Visible response:** score, per-outcome breakdown, explanations, retry set.
- **Accessibility:** form labels, fieldsets, focus management, no colour-only status.

## 8. Memory workshop

### Acrostic

**“Tiny Carbon Makes Isomers”**

| Word | Target item |
|---|---|
| Tiny | Tetravalency |
| Carbon | Catenation |
| Makes | Multiple bonds |
| Isomers | Isomerism |

- Order is meaningful: it moves from the carbon atom’s bonding capacity to self-linking, then multiple bonding, then structural variety.
- Recall prompt: “Cover the table. Rebuild the four reasons from the sentence.”
- The sentence is a real sentence, not an acronym.

### Additional aids

- Comparison contrast table for organic vs inorganic.
- Visual association: carbon as a four-handed connector that can hold hands with itself.
- Flashcards for definitions and examples.

## 10. Technical architecture

- **Stack:** Vue 3, Vite, npm. JavaScript, `<script setup>` single-file components. No router, store library, CSS framework, or remote assets.
- **Application structure:**
  - `src/App.vue` — shell, chapter navigation, progress, section composition.
  - `src/components/lessons/` — one component per lesson.
  - `src/components/` — reusable quick checks, labs, flashcards, revision, assessment, performance report.
  - `src/data/` — question bank, sorter items, everyday items, flashcards, chapter metadata.
  - `src/composables/useProgress.js` — localStorage-backed progress state.
  - `src/lib/scoring.js` — pure scoring and outcome calculations.
  - `src/styles/main.css` — design tokens, typography, layout, shared components.
- **Build:** `npm run build` emits a static `dist/` folder. `vite.config.js` uses `base: './'` so the build can be hosted at a project subpath such as `https://<user>.github.io/<repo>/`.
- **GitHub Pages:** include a GitHub Actions workflow that builds and deploys `dist/` via `actions/deploy-pages`.
- **Offline/local:** after `npm run build`, `dist/` is static and can be served by any static host. The development app requires the Vite dev server.
- **Persistence:** localStorage keys namespaced under `icse10.organic1.*`. Progress is local to the browser only.
- **Companion files:**
  - `chapter-content.md` — full teaching content and mapping.
  - `chapter-questions.json` — structured question bank.
  - `chapter-sources.md` — sources and verification notes.
  - `QA-REPORT.md` — tests performed and limitations.
  - `README.md` — local development, build, and deployment instructions.
  - `AGENTS.md` — project conventions and commands.
